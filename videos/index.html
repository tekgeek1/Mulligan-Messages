Getting started

Two minutes to set up

Step 1

Build your bag

Tell Mulligan which clubs you carry. It uses them for club suggestions and tracks how far you hit each one.

  • Profile → Manage Golf Bag
  • Tap the circle next to a club to add it or take it out
  • Tap a club to set its brand and model
  • Carry two of the same type? Use Add Club

On the course

Play it your way

Four modes

Pick how much you want the app to do

Switch any time during a round from the button at the bottom of the screen. Your score stays.

  • GPS + Tracking: clubs, shots and automatic scoring
  • GPS + Scorecard: distances, you keep score
  • Rangefinder: just distances, no score
  • Scorecard: no GPS, just score

What's new

Rolling out soon

Coming soon

Use your own club distances

Already know how far you hit each club? Type it in. It stays separate from the average Mulligan calculates, so one never overwrites the other.

  • Manage Golf Bag → tap a club
  • Turn on "Use my own distance"
  • Turn it off to go back to automatic

Coming soon

Distance pin goes where you tap

Measure to a bunker, hazard or the edge of a green. The pin lands exactly under your finger, and you can drag it to fine-tune.

Coming soon

Review Shots

After a hole, check the shots Mulligan tracked. Change a wrong club, move a shot, or add one it missed.

  • An orange warning marks a shot that looks off
  • Optional. Tap Next Hole to skip it

Talk to me

A real person reads this

How I handle requests

Mulligan Pro is built by one golfer. Your emails go straight to me, and they decide what gets built next.

  1. You send it. A feature idea, a course that's missing, or something that looks wrong on the map.
  2. I read it and try to reproduce it. For courses, I check the scorecard and the green locations against real sources.
  3. It goes on the list. Things that come up more than once move to the top.
  4. You hear back. When it ships, it's in the release notes, and I'll reply to you if you wrote in.

The scorecard preview and several course fixes started as emails from golfers.
